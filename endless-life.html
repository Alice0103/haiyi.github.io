Project 03 — Adaptive Reuse · Melbourne

Endless
Life

Banana Alley Vaults — a dialogue between the old and the new

A

Strata of history,
reassembled

This project aims to adaptively reuse the Banana Alley Vaults, a heritage site located between the Birrarung River and Flinders Street, transforming it into a public pedestrian passage and art space.

Adhering to the Victorian Heritage Conservation Law, the design utilizes recycled materials — including red brick, traditional lime mortar, stone, and construction waste from modern buildings — to preserve the site's original character while promoting sustainability.

The design concept is rooted in creating a dialogue between the old and the new by juxtaposing historic elements with modern urban materials, such as stained glass embedded in concrete and brick structures. This contrast invites viewers to reflect on the layered history and evolving identity of the city.

"Discarded bricks and stones are reassembled into partition walls — strata of history, to be reinterpreted."
C

The journey

This storyboard maps the visitor's journey through the exhibition, where each interaction with installations reveals the material's unique qualities. By engaging with recycled products and their re-creation, the exhibition evolves into a temporary maker space — an environment of experimentation and collective making. At the same time, the art corridor functions as a public passage that connects the interior space with the Yarra River, extending the dialogue between material reuse and the city's cultural landscape.

B

The installations

The entrance installation is built from recycled urban waste materials, introducing the narrative of the corridor. Light emanates from within the structure, projecting dynamic patterns onto the ground and guiding visitors into the space.

By engaging with recycled products and their re-creation, the exhibition evolves into a temporary maker space — an environment of experimentation and collective making. At the same time, the art corridor functions as a public passage that connects the interior space with the Yarra River, extending the dialogue between material reuse and the city's cultural landscape.

In the stained-glass installation, recycled industrial materials support a Gothic-style frame, where light transforms the object into both structure and memory. The projection on the wall blurs the line between past and present, material and immaterial, extending the project's theme of dialogue across time and space.
